# Derek walkthrough: make an edit, see it in Valheim

This is a roughly five-minute creator-workflow review. The retained Integration
Practice campaign has three encounters. Before inviting Derek, the operator must
stage the archived release, select `integration-melee`, start the campaign, and
verify the starting view. AM4 is currently restored and closed.

1. **Start outside the Field Lodge in daylight.** Armor, food and the godsword are
   already equipped. Read **First Encounter** and its instruction at the top of
   the screen. The lone sign and chest sit to the left, clear of the fighting path.
   The chest opens with **E** and contains spare spears; no pickup is needed for this lap.
2. **Let the Draugr approach and left-click.** Read the completion response. The
   next quest starts automatically. Strike the next Greyling, then the third.
   This demonstrates conditions, progression and authored feedback.
3. **In DMos, open Integration Practice.** The measured lodge appears beside the
   encounters. Expand **View recorded result** to see each defeated creature,
   finishing condition and actual response. The scene is saved geometry; live
   quest results are listed separately.
4. **Choose Edit this hunt under Your Third Encounter.** In Studio's Create/Edit
   form, change Greyling to Draugr, keep the melee finish, and write an instruction
   and response you will recognize. Save. In DMos, use **Preview campaign replay**,
   then **Confirm campaign replay** and accept its displayed scope.
5. **Play the three encounters again.** The last enemy and response should now be
   yours. Inspect its recorded result in DMos. Earlier attempts keep their own
   authored versions. The preserved video also shows creating and placing this
   third hunt through Studio from the earlier two-hunt campaign.

Useful human feedback: Was the goal clear on arrival? Did the next quest read as
progress? Could you find the editor and predict what your change would do? Did
seeing your own enemy and response in the game make the workflow understandable?

The automated lap has already checked damage events, completion, wrong-finisher
rejection, restart, replay, exact scene linkage and recovery. The operator handles
restaging and cleanup; Derek's seat time is for clarity and feel.
